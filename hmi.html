HMI & IVI features

What every car screen and feature does, how to test it, and which bugs are typical. When the interviewer asks "How would you test X?", the answer is in this chapter.

BeginnerReading time: 45 minOften asked: telltales, warnings, "how would you test…"
In very simple words
  • HMI = everything the driver sees, hears and touches.
  • Cluster = the display behind the steering wheel (speed, warnings). Head unit = the centre screen (music, phone, maps).
  • Warning light colours: red = danger, amber = warning, green = something is on.
  • For every feature, test 3 things: does it work? does it look right? does it survive problems (a call, a restart, a lost signal)?

Think of it like testing a mobile phone app, but in a moving car. A mistake can distract or even endanger the driver, so the rules are stricter.

What HMI and IVI mean

Simple idea

HMI (Human–Machine Interface) = every way the driver and the car communicate: screens, buttons, touch, voice, sounds, warning lamps. IVI (In-Vehicle Infotainment) = the "information + entertainment" part, mainly the centre screen: music, phone, maps, apps, settings.

Display / partWhereWhat it showsWhy testers care
Instrument clusterBehind the steering wheelSpeed, RPM, gear, fuel/battery, range, odometer, outside temp, telltales, warningsSafety-relevant: wrong speed or a missing warning is critical
Centre display (head unit)Middle of the dashboardMedia, radio, phone, navigation, climate, settings, apps, cameraMost features and most user complaints
Head-up display (HUD)Projected on the windscreenSpeed, navigation arrows, speed limit, warningsMust match the cluster exactly
Passenger / rear displaysFront passenger or back seatsMedia, climate for rearMust not distract the driver (e.g. video)
ControlsSteering-wheel buttons, rotary knob, touch, hardkeysInput to the HMIEach input path must work: touch AND buttons AND voice
AudioSpeakersChimes, warnings, navigation voice, music, callsSound priority: a warning must be heard over music

The instrument cluster and telltales

A telltale is a small lamp symbol on the cluster. Colours have fixed meanings (standard ISO 2575):

ColourMeaningExamples
RedDanger / stop now / serious problemSeatbelt not fastened, brake system fault, airbag fault, oil pressure, battery/charging fault, door open
Amber / yellowWarning / check soon / a system is limitedEngine check (MIL), ABS fault, tyre pressure (TPMS), low fuel, ESC off / ESC active (flashing)
GreenA function is on and workingTurn indicators, low beam, cruise control active, "Ready" in EVs
BlueHigh beam onHigh beam
White / greyInformation, standbyCruise control standby, set speed

How to test a telltale (complete checklist)

  1. Correct symbol: the right icon from the spec/ISO 2575.
  2. Correct colour: red, amber, green, blue as specified.
  3. Correct position: in its fixed place on the cluster.
  4. ON condition: turns on exactly when the signal says so (e.g. SeatbeltDriver = unbuckled and speed > X).
  5. OFF condition: turns off when the condition ends.
  6. Blinking: blink rate if it blinks. Turn indicators must flash about 90 ± 30 times per minute (UN R48).
  7. Bulb check: at ignition ON, telltales light briefly to prove they work.
  8. Timing: reaction time from signal to display within the spec (e.g. < 200 ms).
  9. Signal faults: invalid value (SNA) or message timeout → defined fault behaviour, never stuck.
  10. Priority / overlap: with many telltales on, nothing overlaps or hides another.
  11. Day / night brightness: readable in both.
  12. Persistence: after an ignition cycle, the telltale comes back if the problem still exists.
Say this: "Explain your experience with telltale testing"

"For each telltale I verify symbol, colour and position against the spec, then the on and off conditions by simulating the input signals in CANoe. I check blinking frequency, the bulb check at ignition on, reaction time, and what happens when the signal is invalid or times out. I also test several telltales together for priority and overlap, and I repeat critical ones after an ignition cycle."

Speed display rules (good to know)

  • UN R39: the speedometer may show more than the true speed but never less. (Displayed speed ≤ 110% of true speed + 4 km/h.)
  • The needle, digital value, HUD and any other speed display must agree with each other.
  • Units follow the settings (km/h vs mph) everywhere at once.

Warnings, pop-ups and chimes

Besides telltales, the cluster shows warning messages (pop-ups) like "Tyre pressure low" and plays chimes (warning sounds).

  • Priority: warnings have levels (e.g. critical > warning > info). A critical warning must interrupt anything else on screen and any music.
  • Queueing: if 3 warnings come at once, they are shown in priority order; none is lost.
  • Acknowledge: some can be closed with OK; critical ones may not be closable.
  • Re-appear: a closed warning comes back after a time or an ignition cycle if the problem still exists.
  • Chime rules: correct sound, volume (not silenced by the media volume), repetition, stops when the problem is gone.
  • Audio ducking: music gets quieter (or pauses) while a warning or navigation voice plays.
Real example: seatbelt warning

Spec: "If speed > 20 km/h and the driver seatbelt is unbuckled, show the red seatbelt telltale and play a chime for 90 s." Tests: 19/20/21 km/h (boundaries), buckled/unbuckled, seat empty (passenger side), chime stops after 90 s, chime stops immediately when buckled, loud music playing, ignition cycle while unbuckled.

The "5 directions" method: how to test ANY feature

Simple idea

When someone asks "How would you test X?", don't panic. Walk through these five directions and you will always have a structured, complete answer.

DirectionQuestion to ask yourselfExample for Bluetooth phone calls
1. Normal useDoes it work the way a normal user uses it?Pair phone, make a call, receive a call, end it
2. BoundariesWhat happens at the limits (min, max, just over)?Max number of paired phones; 5,000 contacts; very long names
3. InterruptionsWhat if something else happens in the middle?Call during navigation voice; reverse gear during call; second incoming call
4. Errors / negativeWhat if something goes wrong?Phone out of range, Bluetooth switched off on phone, wrong PIN, battery empty
5. Power / state changesWhat about ignition cycles, sleep, low voltage?Auto-reconnect after ignition off/on; call active while ignition goes off

Feature by feature: what to test and classic bugs

Classic bugs: contacts missing or duplicated after sync; names with umlauts broken; echo; call drops when switching to CarPlay; phone doesn't reconnect after sleep; call timer wrong; ringtone plays over the navigation prompt.

Classic bugs: progress bar doesn't match the time; wrong cover art; shuffle repeats songs; crash on corrupt file; resumes the wrong track; text in the wrong language.

3. Radio (FM / AM / DAB)

  • Frequency band limits: FM 87.5–108.0 MHz (steps of 0.1 or 0.05), AM (kHz). Nothing outside the band.
  • Seek up/down, scan, manual tuning, presets (save, recall, overwrite), station list.
  • RDS: station name and radio text; long text must scroll or be shortened, not overflow.
  • TA (traffic announcements) interrupt music and go back afterwards.
  • DAB ↔ FM switching when the digital signal is weak.
  • Classic bugs: preset highlights the wrong station; frequency shown with wrong unit or format; RDS text overflows; no audio after TA.

4. Navigation

  • Destination entry: address, POI, favourites, voice; special characters (Straße, ä).
  • Route calculation, alternatives, reroute when you leave the route.
  • Turn-by-turn guidance: arrow on centre screen, cluster and HUD must match, voice prompt timing, lane guidance.
  • ETA and remaining distance/time; units km/miles; 12/24-hour time.
  • No GPS (tunnel, garage); map updates; traffic info.
  • Classic bugs: arrow shows right, text says left; ETA in the past; street name cut off; wrong units; lane guidance doesn't highlight the lane.

5. Apple CarPlay / Android Auto (phone projection)

  • Wired and wireless connection; first connection and reconnection.
  • Switching between native car screens and projection; home button; reverse camera must still appear.
  • Audio focus: phone music vs car radio vs navigation voice vs calls.
  • Steering-wheel buttons and voice (Siri/Google Assistant) work.
  • Disconnect during navigation or a call; phone OS updates.
  • Classic bugs: black screen; audio stays muted after a call; no reconnect after sleep; touch offset.

6. Climate (HVAC)

  • Temperature range and step (e.g. 16–28 °C in 0.5 steps, plus LO/HI) — perfect for BVA.
  • °C/°F setting everywhere; driver/passenger sync.
  • Fan levels (e.g. 1–7), AUTO, A/C, recirculation, MAX defrost (usually cancels AUTO).
  • Seat heating/ventilation levels; steering-wheel heating.
  • Screen and physical buttons stay in sync.
  • Classic bugs: mixed units; values outside the range; two conflicting modes active; screen shows ON but the function is OFF.

7. Rear camera & parking (PDC)

  • Camera appears quickly after selecting reverse. US rule FMVSS 111: within 2 seconds. Also test right after ignition on (boot time).
  • Guide lines move with the steering angle; static and dynamic lines.
  • Parking sensors: bars and beep speed match the distance; front/rear/side.
  • Camera has priority over other screens (CarPlay, pop-ups).
  • Camera image freeze must be detected (not showing an old image).
  • Classic bugs: camera late or black; frozen image; pop-up covers camera; lines don't move.

8. Voice control

  • Commands: "Call Anna", "Navigate home", "Temperature 21 degrees".
  • Accents, noise (open window, fan at max), cancel, timeout, languages.
  • Music ducked while listening; correct feedback on screen.

9. Settings & localisation (language)

  • Language change: every text changes, nothing is left in the old language, no text cut off (German words are long!), right-to-left languages (Arabic, Hebrew) mirrored correctly.
  • Units: km/miles, °C/°F, 12/24 h, date format (30.09.2026 vs 09/30/2026), decimal comma vs point.
  • Date/time: manual and GPS/network time; time zones; daylight saving.
  • Profiles: settings saved per driver; factory reset clears everything (including phone pairings).

10. Driver-distraction restrictions

Many functions are blocked while driving for safety: video playback, typing on the keyboard, long lists. Test: at speed below the limit they work; above it they are blocked with a clear message; passenger screen may still play video.

11. Software update / OTA

  • Update download, installation, progress, cancel, power loss during update, rollback if it fails, version shown after update.
  • Settings and pairings kept after update.

12. Electric-car (EV) screens

  • Battery % (state of charge, SoC), battery bar, range; they must agree.
  • Charging: plug connected, power (kW), time to full (hours and minutes!), charge limit.
  • Charging stops on the navigation route; battery at arrival.
  • Logic: the car cannot be charging from a charger while driving.

Look and feel: the UI checklist

These are the things you check on every screen (this is also the method for the spot-the-defect drills):

Text

Spelling, language mix, cut-off text, placeholder text ("Hello", "TBD", "STR_ID_12").

Units & formats

km/mi, °C/°F, date and time format, decimal separator, impossible values (30 February, 25:10, 00:75).

Consistency

The same value is shown the same way everywhere (needle vs number, cluster vs HUD).

Icons & colours

Right icon for the label; colour meaning (red/amber/green).

Layout

Overlap, alignment, cut-off, tilted images, missing items.

State

The highlighted button matches the current screen or mode.

Logic

Impossible combinations (charging while driving, ETA in the past).

Touch & feedback

Touch area big enough, press feedback, no double action, scroll works.

Quick answers for the interview

What is the difference between HMI and IVI?

HMI is everything the driver uses to interact with the car: displays, buttons, touch, voice, sounds, warning lamps. IVI is the infotainment part of it, mainly the centre screen with media, phone, navigation and apps. The cluster is HMI but not really infotainment.

How would you test an HMI warning when a sensor fails or is disconnected?

I would simulate the failure in the signal simulator in three ways: send the "signal not available" value, stop sending the message so it times out, and set the sensor fault flag if there is one. On a HIL bench I could also disconnect the real sensor. Expected, per spec: the HMI shows the fault indication (for example dashes or an amber fault telltale with a message), does not freeze on the old value, does not crash, a DTC is stored, and everything recovers when the signal comes back. I also check the timing of the fault detection.

How would you test Bluetooth phone pairing?

I use five directions. Normal: pair from the car and from the phone, with iPhone and Android, confirm the PIN. Boundaries: maximum number of paired devices, very long device names. Interruptions: incoming call during pairing, ignition off during pairing. Negative: wrong PIN, Bluetooth off on the phone, phone out of range, cancel pairing. Power: auto-reconnect after an ignition cycle and after sleep. I also check the phonebook sync and the status icons.

What types of HMI components have you tested?

Answer with your real experience, grouped: "On the cluster: [telltales, warnings, speed and fuel displays]. On the head unit: [media, Bluetooth phone, radio, navigation, climate, settings, camera]. And cross-cutting topics like language switching, units and ignition-cycle behaviour."
